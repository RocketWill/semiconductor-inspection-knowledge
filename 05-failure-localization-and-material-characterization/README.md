# Failure Localization and Material Characterization

The previous section focused on process signals, WAT, and CP results. Here the question changes: once an electrical or spatial anomaly has been narrowed down, what can different physical signals tell me about the suspected structure?

## Notes

1. [Layout-Induced Failures: Antenna Effect and Latch-Up](./01-layout-induced-failures-antenna-and-latchup.md) — how layout can create electrical risk during plasma processing or through parasitic CMOS paths.
2. [Emission Microscopy and Electrical Localization](./02-emission-microscopy-and-electrical-localization.md) — how photon emission under bias can narrow abnormal electrical activity to a smaller region.
3. [Reading SEM and EDS Signals](./03-electron-beam-signals-sem-and-eds.md) — how SE, BSE, and characteristic X-rays answer different questions about surface geometry, contrast, and candidate elements.
4. [FIB Cross-Sections, TEM Preparation, and Voltage Contrast](./04-fib-tem-preparation-and-voltage-contrast.md) — how FIB opens a buried structure or prepares a targeted lamella, and how voltage contrast adds an electrical clue.

## How I Read These Methods

Each method answers a different question. EMMI helps narrow an electrically active location; SEM and EDS add structural and elemental evidence; FIB exposes a buried region or prepares a targeted TEM lamella; voltage contrast helps compare electrical behavior.

The useful part is how these results connect, rather than expecting one image to finish the failure analysis.
